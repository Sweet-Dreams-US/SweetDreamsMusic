When a song comes out, the credits list a few names that most listeners skip: producer, mixer, mastering engineer. Each one has a different job, and each job happens at a different stage. If you are an artist planning a release, knowing who does what helps you budget, ask the right questions and make sure everyone who contributed is credited and paid.

This article covers the three roles in plain terms, then explains how some of these people can earn royalties after a song is released.

## The producer

The producer role varies more than the other two. On one record the producer writes parts and directs the performances. On another the producer builds the beat and hands over files. On a third the producer mostly keeps the sessions moving and makes decisions about the sound. There is no single definition that fits every project.

Because of that, the most useful step an artist can take is to agree on the producer's role in writing before work starts. Decide what the producer will deliver, who owns what, and how the producer will be paid. Berklee Online lists "featured artist and producer advances and royalties" as its own lesson in a course on music business revenue streams.

## The recording stage

Before mixing, the parts have to be captured. The Yamaha Hub explains that recording means capturing every instrument and vocal part, as audio or MIDI, onto separate tracks in recording software. It names three kinds of recording work. Tracking captures the foundation, such as drums, bass and rhythm parts, usually first. Overdubbing adds new parts over existing tracks. Live recording has a band play together.

The same article notes that hip-hop and electronic music often skip tracking, because the foundation is programmed with loops or MIDI. That is one reason the producer's job looks so different from one genre to the next.

## The mixer

Mixing turns many separate tracks into one finished stereo file. Yamaha Hub describes it as combining the multitrack recording into a mono or stereo file, a step called the mix-down. The mixer sets the level of each track, places it left or right in the stereo field, and adds effects such as reverb and delay. Automation lets the mixer change levels, placement and effects at specific points in the song.

In practice that means choices such as how loud the vocal sits against the guitar and where each sound sits in the stereo field. The final step, called bouncing to disk, sums everything into a single file.

:::note Mixing is a separate step from mastering
Yamaha Hub states that engineers who record and mix generally do not master, and that professional mastering engineers usually work in specialized, acoustically tuned facilities.
:::

## The mastering engineer

Mastering is the last stage before release. According to Yamaha Hub, it applies frequency and level adjustments to make the mix sound as good as possible. Mastering engineers use equalization and compression or limiting, usually in small amounts. They also make sure the level suits the delivery format, whether that is streaming, vinyl or CD.

For an album or an EP, the mastering engineer balances the levels between songs so the project plays evenly from start to finish. For CD or vinyl, the engineer also sequences the songs and sets the spacing between them. The artist or producer may attend a mastering session to weigh in on artistic choices such as the gaps between songs.

## How these roles can get paid after release

Most people know that artists and songwriters can earn royalties. Fewer know that producers, mixers and engineers can also share in certain royalties, and that it usually has to be set up in advance.

The U.S. Copyright Office explains that Title III of the Music Modernization Act lets music producers, mixers and sound engineers receive royalties collected for uses of sound recordings under the section 114 statutory license. SoundExchange, the designated collective, distributes those royalties under what is called a letter of direction.

SoundExchange describes a letter of direction as a form in which a featured artist tells SoundExchange to send part of the artist's sound recording performance royalties to a creative participant, such as a producer, engineer or mixer. Session musicians and background singers are not eligible payees on this form. The artist or the artist's authorized signatory signs it, and a repertoire chart lists each recording and the percentage.

:::note A few facts about letters of direction
The percentage is entered as a percentage of the featured artist's share. A letter only affects the featured artist who signs it, so on a recording with several featured artists, each one needs to sign for the payee to be paid from each share. SoundExchange says processing takes a minimum of two weeks for a complete submission. Source: SoundExchange.
:::

SoundExchange does not state a standard percentage on that page. The split is something the artist and the creative participant agree on. That is why it is worth settling before the song is released, and not after money starts coming in.

## Questions to ask before you hire

Whether you are working with a producer, a mixer or a mastering engineer, a few questions keep things clear for everyone.

What exactly will be delivered, and in what file format? How many rounds of changes are included? Who owns the finished files? Will the person be credited, and will they be named in a letter of direction? If a song is going to streaming, what level and format does the final master need to meet?

Putting the answers in a short written agreement protects both the artist and the person doing the work. It also keeps the credits accurate, which matters when royalties are paid out later.

## The short version

The producer shapes the record as a whole, with a role that changes from project to project. The mixer balances the recorded tracks into one stereo file. The mastering engineer prepares that file for release and for the format it will be heard on. Producers, mixers and engineers may be paid royalties through a letter of direction signed by the featured artist, and that paperwork is best done early.
